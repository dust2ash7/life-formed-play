Creek shelf

Grasses

Ten native grasses. Blade on top, seed below. None of them belong in the flowing water.

Moist shelf

  • Creeping wildrye

    Elymus triticoides

    Spreads by the root and knits the shelf so muddy water slows down.

    2–3 ft · Moist · Plug

  • Meadow barley

    Hordeum brachyantherum

    A shorter cool-season grass for the damp meadow, not the channel.

    1–2 ft · Moist · Plug

  • Deergrass

    Muhlenbergia rigens

    The fountain clump. Seed heads feed birds. Moist ground to nearly dry.

    3 ft · Moist to dry · Plug

Under the willow

  • Blue wildrye

    Elymus glaucus

    Upright, and happier in willow shade than in the open sun.

    2–4 ft · Shade · Plug

  • California brome

    Bromus carinatus

    Quick cover from seed. It is short-lived, then it reseeds itself.

    2–3 ft · Shade to sun · Seed

  • Coast range melic

    Melica imperfecta

    Fine blades on a rocky bank in the shade. Not for wet feet.

    1–2 ft · Dry shade · Plug

Dry upper edge

  • Purple needlegrass

    Stipa pulchra

    The state grass. Deep roots on the dry upper edge.

    2 ft · Dry · Seed

  • Foothill needlegrass

    Stipa lepida

    Finer than purple needlegrass, and it likes a little more water. Still above the wet band.

    1–2 ft · Dry edge · Seed

  • Giant wildrye

    Elymus condensatus

    Wide blue blades and a tall clump. Keep it on the dry bench, not in the creek.

    4–6 ft · Dry bench · Plug

  • Purple threeawn

    Aristida purpurea

    A dry rocky grass with a purple seed head. Upper edge only.

    1–2 ft · Dry rock · Seed

Plant November through February. Clumps go in as plugs. Needlegrass, threeawn, and brome can be seed. Do not mow to the water. Do not plant fountain grass, pampas, Bermuda, or kikuyu.
