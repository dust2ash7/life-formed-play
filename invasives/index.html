Creek shelf

Invasive plants

Ten plants that do not belong here. Leaf on top, the mark below. If the mark is missing, leave the plant.

In the water

  • Watercress

    Nasturtium officinale

    A mat in slow water. Each leaf is split into leaflets, and the flowers are tiny and white.

    Not yerba mansa. That one sits on the damp bank, with whole oval leaves.

    A mat · Still water · Split leaf

Wet bank

  • Giant reed

    Arundo donax

    A woody cane, thick as a thumb, with joints and a wide leaf. The top ends in a pale plume.

    Not cattail, and not tule. Cattail is a brown sausage on a strap leaf. Tule is a plain round stem.

    10–20 ft · Channel and bank · Jointed cane

  • Tamarisk

    Tamarix ramosissima

    Feathery shrub. The leaf is a tiny scale, like cedar, and the flowers are pink sprays.

    Not willow. Willow leaves are long and simple, and the flowers are catkins, not pink mist.

    A shrub · Wet bank · Scale leaf

  • Castor bean

    Ricinus communis

    A big hand-shaped leaf, often a red stem, and a spiny round seed pod. Nothing native here looks like it.

    Not a young cottonwood. Those leaves are more heart-shaped, and they do not carry a spiny ball.

    6–10 ft · Damp bank · Spiny pod

  • Mexican fan palm

    Washingtonia robusta

    A stiff fan of pleated leaves. Older ones grow a trunk with a skirt of dead fans.

    Not a young willow, and not tule. A fan on a hard stalk is a palm, and it does not belong in this creek.

    A trunk · Creek bed · Fan leaf

Dry edge

  • Pampas grass

    Cortaderia selloana

    A huge clump. The leaf edge cuts skin. The plume is a feather duster, white or pink, held high.

    Not deergrass. Deergrass has thin blades that do not cut, and a narrow tan spike.

    6–10 ft · Dry edge · Cuts the hand

  • Fountain grass

    Cenchrus setaceus

    A smaller clump with arching stems. The seed head is a soft pink or purple bottlebrush.

    Not purple threeawn. Threeawn is wiry hairs, not a fluffy brush.

    3–4 ft · Dry edge · Bottlebrush

  • Bermuda grass

    Cynodon dactylon

    A low wiry mat. Runners lie on top of the soil. The seed head is a star of thin fingers.

    Not creeping wildrye. Wildrye stands knee-high, and the seed looks like a slim head of wheat.

    Ankle high · The path · Star of fingers

  • Kikuyu grass

    Cenchrus clandestinus

    A thick mat with wider blades. The runner is fat and pale, and the flowers hide down in the leaves.

    Not creeping wildrye. If you can see a wheat-like head above the leaves, it is the wildrye.

    A mat · The path · Fat pale runner

  • Tree tobacco

    Nicotiana glauca

    A soft gray-green shrub. Yellow tubes cluster at the branch tips. A crushed leaf smells sharp.

    Not willow. Willow is greener, the leaf is longer, and it does not flower in yellow tubes.

    6–12 ft · Dry bank · Yellow tubes

Jubata grass matches pampas: a cutting leaf and a huge plume. A cane thinner than a thumb may be a reed, not giant reed. When you are unsure, leave it. Take confirmed weeds by hand in fall or winter, and keep every piece out of the water.
