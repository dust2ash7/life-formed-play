Creek shelf

Rock and soil

The ground underfoot. Color on top, the mark underneath. Stone is one cobble, not a wall.

What you walk

  • Shelf soil

    The warm ground

    The tan you walk on. It holds once roots are in it.

    The path · Dries on top · Leave it

  • Wet soil

    The dark band

    Darker at the waterline. It stays damp after the creek drops.

    A hand wide · Stays damp · Leave it

  • Loose dirt

    Unbound soil

    The patches you clear. If it stays, the next rain takes it into the creek.

    The bare spots · Washes in · Clear it

  • Leaf duff

    Under the willow

    A thin dark layer of leaves. It feeds the creek. Do not dig it out.

    A thin layer · Stays damp · Leave it

Under the shelf

  • Decomposed granite

    The local grit

    Gritty sand under many San Diego creeks. It drains fast. Do not dump it in the channel.

    Under the soil · Drains · Leave it

  • Clay

    Sticky ground

    Holds water and slumps when it is bare. Keep plants on it.

    Pockets · Holds · Cover it

Stone

  • Dry cobble

    Stone on the shelf

    Lighter, because it has dried. The creek rolled it, then left it up here.

    Fist-size · Dry · Leave it

  • Wet cobble

    Stone in the water

    Darker, because it stays wet. Moss only shows in the damp crack.

    Fist-size · Always wet · Leave it

  • Moss seam

    The green crack

    A thin green line where rock meets damp soil. It arrives. Do not buy a sheet of it.

    A crack · Damp · Let it come

  • Set stone

    One placed cobble

    The single stone you press into the shelf. One is enough. A line of them is a wall.

    One · On the shelf · Place it

Clear only the loose dirt. Leave the duff and the cobbles. Do not haul in fill, gravel, or decomposed granite. Do not pave the bed. One set stone is the whole rock job.
