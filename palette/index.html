Creek shelf

Native palette

Twelve plants for a San Diego buffer. Leaf on top, flower or seed below.

Streamside

  • Arroyo willow

    Salix lasiolepis

    Shades the creek and holds the bank with its roots.

    10–20 ft · Wet toes · Winter cutting

  • Mulefat

    Baccharis salicifolia

    The shrub that stands with the willow. Fast to root, hard to wash out.

    6–10 ft · Wet to moist · Winter cutting

  • Southern cattail

    Typha domingensis

    Tall blades in the shallows. They slow the water so mud drops.

    6–9 ft · Standing water · Plug

  • Pale spikerush

    Eleocharis macrostachya

    A fine green rush at the waterline. This is the reed on the shelf.

    1–3 ft · Shallow water · Plug

Damp bank

  • San Diego sedge

    Carex spissa

    A wide blue-green clump for a damp bank. The local one.

    3–4 ft · Damp soil · Plug

  • Yerba mansa

    Anemopsis californica

    A low white flower that mats wet ground. Leaves redden in the fall.

    Under 1 ft · Wet soil · Plug

  • Scarlet monkeyflower

    Erythranthe cardinalis

    A red flower where the bank stays moist. The bright note on the shelf.

    2–3 ft · Stays moist · Plug

  • California wild rose

    Rosa californica

    A thicket with a pink flower and red hips. Cover for birds, not a wall.

    4–6 ft · Moist shelf · Pot

Outer grass

  • Deergrass

    Muhlenbergia rigens

    A fountain of silver-green. Catches muddy water before the creek.

    3 ft · Moist to dry · Plug

  • Creeping wildrye

    Elymus triticoides

    A blue-green grass that spreads and knits the shelf together.

    2–3 ft · Moist to dry · Plug

  • Purple needlegrass

    Stipa pulchra

    Fine blades and a purple seed head. Keep it up where the ground dries.

    2 ft · Dry edge · Seed

  • Blue-eyed grass

    Sisyrinchium bellum

    A small blue star on iris-like leaves. The flower this coast can grow.

    1 ft · Winter-wet · Plug

Plant November through February. Willow and mulefat go in as dormant cuttings. The rest go in as plugs, except needlegrass, which can be seed. No fertilizer. Do not plant giant reed, pampas grass, or tamarisk.
